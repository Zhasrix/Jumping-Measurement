Technical Specification & Biomechanical Whitepaper

IMU Vertical Jump Kinematic Architecture

Algorithmic formulation, signal segmentation, and discrete numerical integration

1.0 Motion Segmentation State Machine

The smartphone is held statically against the chest (sternum) or held in the dominant hand. Automatic segmentation relies on four discrete temporal boundaries:

  • Quiet Baseline ($T_0$ to $T_{quiet}$): 1.5 seconds of stationary stance calibrates gravity vector magnitude $|\vec{g}_{static}| = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2 + a_z^2} \approx 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$ and gyro alignment.
  • Unweighting & Propulsion ($T_{dip}$): Countermovement drop causes net acceleration $a_{net} < -0.2g$, followed immediately by vigorous propulsion where $a_{net} > +1.5g$.
  • Takeoff Instant ($T_{to}$): Exact microsecond when foot contact breaks. Under true freefall, the accelerometer sensor experiences weightlessness: $|\vec{a}_{raw}| < 0.25g$ (approx $2.45\text{ m/s}^2$) for $\ge 60\text{ ms}$.
  • Landing Impact ($T_{land}$): Ground reaction contact generates an abrupt deceleration shock: $|\vec{a}_{raw}| > 2.50g$ ($24.5\text{ m/s}^2$) with jerk derivative $\frac{da}{dt} > 120\text{ g/s}$.

2.0 Dual Kinematic Formulations

Method A: Flight Time Equation

Assuming symmetrical ballistic flight trajectory under standard gravitational acceleration $g = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$:

h_{flight} = \frac{1}{8} \cdot g \cdot (t_{flight})^2 = \frac{1}{8} \cdot 9.80665 \cdot (T_{land} - T_{to})^2

Reliable and unaffected by accelerometer bias drift during flight. Susceptible to knee flexion / tucking on landing.

Method B: Double Integration (Impulse-Momentum)

Trapezoidal numerical integration of net vertical acceleration $a_{net}(t) = |\vec{a}(t)| - g_{bias}$ from initiation of propulsion:

v(t) = \int_{T_{dip}}^{T_{to}} a_{net}(t) dt \implies h_{v0} = \frac{(v_{takeoff})^2}{2g}

Provides true center-of-mass work-energy kinematics, immune to landing knee tuck discrepancies.

3.0 Digital Filtering & Error Mitigation

Smartphone internal MEMS IMUs exhibit high-frequency electrical jitter, tilt rotation during jump, and low-frequency thermal drift. KineticJump Pro integrates a real-time native 1D Kalman state estimator:

// Predict Step
P_{k|k-1} = P_{k-1|k-1} + Q_process; // Q = 0.008 (Process Variance)
// Update Step
K_k = P_{k|k-1} / (P_{k|k-1} + R_measure); // R = 0.055 (Sensor Jitter Covariance)
x_k = x_{k-1} + K_k * (measurement_k - x_{k-1});
P_k = (1 - K_k) * P_{k|k-1};
